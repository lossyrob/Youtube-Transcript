# Rethinking Engineer Compensation in the AI Era: The Story Point Model
**Speaker**: Arman Hezarkhani (Co-founder & Managing Partner, 10x)
**Day**: 1 | **Talk #**: 17

---

## 🎯 Quick Read (TL;DR)

Arman Hezarkhani presents a radical proposition: traditional compensation models (hourly, salary+bonus, equity) are fundamentally misaligned with the AI era. At his company 10x, they compensate engineers like salespeople—paying based on story points completed rather than hours worked or salary. The result? They've attracted top talent including rocket scientists from NASA, shipped high-quality code fast, and created direct incentives for engineers to master AI tools.

The talk frames AI adoption as an incentive problem. That WeWork neighbor typing "like a caveman" while Arman had 45 agents running wasn't less capable—they just had no financial reason to optimize. When you pay engineers per output unit, they naturally seek every efficiency gain possible. 10x uses a strategist/engineer split where strategists scope work (preventing story point inflation) and engineers execute, with multiple QA rounds and client approval creating counterbalances.

**Core Thesis**: Current compensation models (salary, bonus, equity) fail to incentivize AI tool mastery; output-based compensation directly rewards engineers who leverage AI to deliver more, faster.

**Top 3 Takeaways**:
1. 10x pays engineers a flat base plus per-story-point fees—treating code delivery like sales commission
2. "AI makes people look like one of those crazy mirrors"—amplifies both excellence and sloppiness 10x
3. Multiple counterbalances (strategist scoping, internal QA, client approval) prevent gaming the system

## 📋 Key Takeaways

### Main Arguments
- **The Incentive Gap**: Observed a stark contrast in a WeWork—one person typing character-by-character while Arman ran 45 agents simultaneously. The difference wasn't capability but incentive structure. As a founder, he had every reason to maximize efficiency; a salaried employee with annual bonus and equity doesn't have the same urgency.
- **Evolution of Compensation Has Stalled**: Traced compensation history: hourly (no incentive to work faster) → project-based (engineers pad estimates) → salary+bonus (people clock in/out) → equity (most engineers prefer cash, take equity as nice-to-have). None of these directly incentivize tool mastery or output optimization.
- **Direct Output Compensation Works**: 10x has hired incredible engineers (former founders, ML researchers, NASA rocket scientists) who ship maintainable, high-quality code quickly. The model attracts people who believe in their own productivity.
- **Quality Controls Are Essential**: Three safeguards prevent gaming: (1) Strategists scope tickets, not engineers—they're compensated on NR/customer happiness as counterbalance, (2) Multiple rounds of internal QA, (3) Client must accept every ticket before engineer gets paid.
- **Hiring Is The Foundation**: "We make hiring incredibly difficult for ourselves so that everything else is easy." This is especially critical with AI—you need people who can use AI tools responsibly and effectively.

### Announcements & Demos
- **10x Business Model**: Two services—roadmapping (AI transformation consulting) and execution (custom builds with story-point compensation). Clients pay per story point delivered.
- **Case Study 1 - Billboard Company**: Built AI moderation system in 2 weeks achieving 96% accuracy vs. human moderators. Replaced manual content review for billboard ad uploads, reducing costs and increasing revenue velocity.
- **Case Study 2 - Retail Analytics**: Built 5 parallel AI models for retail devices (heat mapping, queue detection, theft detection) by quantizing models to run on low-power chips.

### Technical Details
- **Process Structure**: Product requirements → Architecture design document (where most engineering time goes) → Tickets with story point estimates → Implementation → QA rounds → Client acceptance
- **Compensation Formula**: Flat base salary + quarterly roundup based on completed story points
- **Role Separation**: Strategists (former PMs, engineers) handle client-facing work and scoping; AI engineers handle architecture docs and implementation
- **Moderation Model Performance**: 96% accuracy compared to human moderator, delivered in 2 weeks
- **Retail Device Models**: 5 models running in parallel on low-power chips through quantization

## 🔮 Implications & Predictions

**For Developers**: 
If you're on traditional salary, ask yourself: what's your actual incentive to master AI tools? The efficiency gains go to your employer, not you. Consider roles at companies with output-based compensation or negotiate performance bonuses tied to measurable productivity metrics.

**For AI Tool Builders**: 
The adoption gap isn't always about tool quality—it's about user incentives. Companies might resist better AI tools because they'd expose productivity differences, creating compensation pressure. Tools that help teams measure and reward AI-powered productivity could unlock adoption.

**For Engineering Leaders**: 
This is a provocative model that won't fit every culture, but the core insight is valuable: if you want engineers to invest time learning AI tools, the reward structure needs to reflect that. Consider hybrid models, innovation bonuses, or team-based output incentives that don't create individual competition.

**Contrarian/Surprising Takes**: 
- Paying engineers like salespeople (commission on output) creates better outcomes than startup equity models
- The WeWork observation implies most employed engineers are rationally under-investing in AI tools because there's no personal upside
- Story points—often mocked as meaningless—become the unit of compensation with proper counterbalances
- Sharp-elbow sales culture can be avoided through role separation and multi-party approval
